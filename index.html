Overview

One world, many players

Video world models let you step into a generated world, but usually alone, and usually only to look around. Interverse opens that world to several players at once.

Navigate

Every player walks and looks around with their own camera, steered by the keyboard.

Interact

Any object can be moved by drawing where it should go, right in the player's own view.

Share

What one player does shows up in every other player's view, consistent in space and time.

Why this is hard

On screen, walking and pushing look the same. A camera move slides every pixel, so a drawn path cannot tell whether the object or the viewer moved. Trajectories mined from ordinary videos by point tracking mix the two kinds of motion as well, which makes it hard for a model trained on them to tell the two apart.

What Interverse does

  1. Separates the two motions. Object motion is drawn in the player's current view, and synchronized multi-view video supervises it. Points that move in a static view are object motion, and they are matched into the moving view.
  2. Speaks in pixels. The camera becomes a rendered lattice world and drags become white dots. Together they form one control video that pretrained generators read with few or no architectural changes.
  3. Generates all players together. Every camera lives in one shared frame, and all views are stitched and denoised jointly, trained on a curated mix of single-view and synchronized multi-view videos.
